DATA SELECTION AND PREPROCESSING NOTES
Dataset: cBioPortal study cll_broad_2022 (CLL, Broad, Nat Genet 2022)
Task:    IGHV mutation status (mutated=1 / unmutated=0) from RNA-seq gene expression
Code:    fl/preprocess.py (reads the raw cBioPortal files, join, filter, feature selection,
         splits; run `python -m fl.preprocess` to write data/processed/)

1. BUILDING THE SAMPLE TABLE
   - data_clinical_sample.txt (one row per tumour sample) is joined to data_clinical_patient.txt
     (one row per patient) on PATIENT_ID, so each sample carries its patient's label and cohort.
   - Samples are then filtered to those present as columns in data_mrna_seq_tpm.txt
     (RNA-seq is available for only a subset: 715 of 1,154 samples), and then to those with a
     non-missing IGHV label. The two filters keep the same intersection regardless of order.
   - Result: 700 samples from 696 patients (4 patients contribute two samples).
   - RNA-seq is used even though other assays cover more patients (mutations ~970, copy-number
     ~1,060 of the labelled samples) because gradient inversion needs dense, continuous features
     whose reconstruction can be measured (e.g. cosine similarity).

2. FEATURE SELECTION
   - Expression is transformed to log2(TPM + 1) because raw TPM is extremely skewed.
   - Genes are classified with the GENCODE v19 (hg19) annotation, downloaded once to data/reference/
     (fl/preprocess.py: load_gene_annotation). v19 matches the old gene names in the expression
     file (e.g. RP11-..., IL8) and covers 99.4% of its genes; current databases (Ensembl lookup,
     cBioPortal gene reference) lack names like TXLNG2P, which is how a Y-linked gene slipped
     through an earlier name-based filter.
   - Gene symbols mangled into dates by a spreadsheet ('10-Sep' = SEPT10, '1-Mar' = MARCH1,
     '15-Sep' = SEP15, '1-Dec' = DEC1; 26 symbols) are restored before anything else. All 26 are
     found in GENCODE v19 after the rename. SEPT10 is one of the more label-informative features
     (correlation about -0.67 with IGHV status). Rows sharing a symbol (2,004 rows, 238 symbols)
     keep only the first; none of the selected genes is affected.
   - Genes dropped before selection:
       * immunoglobulin loci (IG_* biotypes and IGH*/IGK*/IGL* names): the label is IGHV mutation
         status, so these genes are a near-proxy for the label (V-gene usage is tied to mutation
         status, and the reads come from the same rearranged locus) and would make the task partly
         circular. Light-chain and constant-region genes (IGK*, IGL*) are dropped too: they are less
         directly tied to the label, but they are clone fingerprints (kappa vs lambda, specific V
         genes) that would fill about a third of the top 100 (32 of 100 if only IGH* were dropped)
         and make patients trivially identifiable, which is undesirable for the privacy attacks.
       * sex-linked genes: every gene on chromosome Y (e.g. RPS4Y1, DDX3Y, KDM5D, TTTY15,
         TXLNG2P) plus XIST/TSIX. Unrelated to the label. X-linked genes are kept: only 5 of the
         earlier top 100 were X-linked and removing them did not change how well sex could be
         predicted.
       * pseudogenes (all *pseudogene biotypes): their reads are often mismapped from the parent
         gene, and some are sex-linked (TXLNG2P).
       * small RNAs by biotype (snRNA, snoRNA, miRNA, misc_RNA, rRNA) and noisy transcripts by
         name: hemoglobin (blood contamination), clone-named transcripts (AC/AL/AP..., RP11-...),
         MIR/SNOR/LINC. Protein-coding genes and other lncRNAs with ordinary names are kept
         (e.g. CLLU1, a known CLL marker, is protein_coding in v19).
       * non-coding RNA expression is not zero (mean log2(TPM+1) about 3-7 in the earlier top 100),
         so these were removed for the reasons above, not because they are unexpressed.
   - Check: after filtering, sex is only weakly predictable from the selected 100 genes
     (5-fold CV AUC 0.60, versus 0.99 when TXLNG2P was included), and a scan of the 3,000
     highest-variance remaining genes finds none with a sex-association AUC of 0.80 or more.
   - From the remaining genes the 100 highest-variance genes are kept (label-free selection).
     This is variance-based gene selection, not PCA: features stay as individual genes so
     reconstructed vectors remain interpretable.
   - The README plans to try N = 50 / 100 / 500 genes.

3. NODES AND SPLITS
   - FL nodes are split by contributing cohort (COHORT). Cohorts with fewer than 40 patients
     (UCSD, MDACC, NHLBI) are pooled into "OTHER". Nodes: ICGC, GCLLSG, DFCI, OTHER.
   - make_splits works at the patient level: all samples of a patient go to the same split, so no
     patient appears in both train and test (important for membership inference, where
     "was this patient in the training set?" must have a clean answer).
   - Outputs: data/processed/samples_joined.csv (the joined table), data/processed/nodes/<node>/
     {train,val}.csv, and data/processed/test.csv (the shared test set, with a NODE column).
   - A shared test set (20% of patients, stratified by cohort and label) is held out before the
     node split so centralized and federated models are evaluated on the same patients.
     The remainder is split within each node into train/val (80/20 of patients, stratified by
     label).
   - Only 4 patients have two samples and both samples share a label and cohort, so patient-level
     grouping has negligible effect on class balance.
   - Keeping both samples of the 4 repeat patients (rather than one sample per patient) was
     considered and kept. Checked whether the two samples are near-duplicates: they are not.
     In standardized 100-gene space the within-patient distances are 9.5-12.8 (correlations
     0.18-0.57), versus a median of 13.8 between random different patients and 8.0 to the
     nearest other patient. Each pair is also from different sources (a CRC- sample plus a
     DFCI-/JB- sample), probably different sequencing batches. So a model that memorizes one
     sample reveals little about the other, and keeping both is harmless; this also gives the
     round figure of 700 samples (from 696 patients).
   - For membership inference, membership is counted per patient, not per sample, so a repeat
     patient in a node's training set is one member.
   - All 4 repeat patients fall in the "OTHER" node (~8 of its 52 samples), so any effect is
     concentrated there. Sample-level annotations such as TUMOR_MOLECULAR_SUBTYPE disagree
     between the two samples for 2 of the 4 patients; the label used (patient-level
     IGHV_MUTATION_STATUS) is unaffected.

4. STANDARDIZATION (z-score, training data only)
   - Centralized: mean/std computed from the pooled training data of all nodes
     (standardize_pooled); applied to pooled validation and test data.
   - Federated: each node uses the mean/std of its own training data (standardize_local); test
     patients are scaled with the statistics of their own cohort's node.
   - Neither uses validation or test data to compute statistics, so there is no leakage.

LIMITATIONS
   - Centralized and federated runs use different standardization, so a small part of any
     performance gap reflects preprocessing, not federation itself. Local statistics from the
     smallest node ("OTHER", ~30 training patients) are noisy.
   - Variance-based gene selection uses all 700 samples (no labels). It is label-free but not
     strictly restricted to training data.
   - Only four usable nodes; the test set (~140 samples) is small, so results should be averaged
     over several seeds.
